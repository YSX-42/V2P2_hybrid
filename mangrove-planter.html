SUB PROJECT 01

Hybrid solution integrating mangrove and concrete planter

An engineered shelter for the vulnerable establishment stage of mangroves—and a living coastal defence that can strengthen as vegetation matures.

Research focus

Protection now. Adaptation over time.

The hybrid system combines modular concrete planters with young mangrove seedlings to reduce wave loading while supporting vegetation establishment.

Laboratory experiments investigate how planter height, submergence, spacing and array density influence wave transmission, reflection and the forces acting on seedlings. The aim is to translate the measured response into practical guidance for grey-green shoreline protection.

WavesPeriodic and solitary conditions
SystemPlanter arrays + seedlings
OutputPerformance relationships

Experimental approach

From geometry to hydrodynamic response.

01

Configure the prototype

Vary planter size, height, submergence, lateral spacing, longitudinal spacing and the presence of young mangrove seedlings.

02

Measure wave response

Track wave transmission, reflection, velocity and turbulence under regular and extreme storm-like wave conditions.

03

Assess local risk

Examine sheltering alongside local flow acceleration, downflow and possible scour near planter edges.

Current evidence

Promising performance, with design trade-offs.

30–70%

Reduced seedling loading

Concrete planters reduced the hydrodynamic forces acting on young seedlings in the reported laboratory tests.

5–17%

Wave-energy reduction

Reported attenuation or dissipation ranges show meaningful wave reduction across tested hybrid-planter configurations.

Depth matters

Submergence influences decay

Planter height relative to water depth is an important design parameter, with stronger decay observed in shallower conditions.

Local flow

Shelter can shift risk

Reduced loading on seedlings must be balanced against accelerated flow and potential sediment erosion around planter edges.
