SUB PROJECT 03

Evolution of bed geomorphology caused by real seagrass

Following the two-way relationship between a living seagrass canopy and the seabed: vegetation alters flow and sediment, while the changing bed reshapes the habitat.

Research focus

Reading the seabed as a record of flow.

Real seagrass is dynamic. Its flexible blades move with waves and currents, changing turbulence and near-bed velocity in ways that rigid models cannot fully reproduce.

This sub-project observes how those living motions influence erosion, deposition and the evolving bed around and within a meadow. The resulting morphology helps reveal when seagrass stabilises sediment—and where local scour may still emerge.

MaterialReal, flexible seagrass
ProcessErosion + deposition
ScalePatch to meadow edge

Observation sequence

Flow, movement, morphology.

01

Establish a living canopy

Arrange real seagrass at controlled density and patch length while maintaining representative blade flexibility.

02

Capture hydrodynamics

Observe wave and current velocities above, within and around the vegetation canopy, including its leading and trailing edges.

03

Map bed evolution

Compare bed levels through time to identify patterns of scour, deposition and sediment redistribution caused by the vegetation.

Why it matters

A living system changes the design question.

Canopy

Flexibility controls resistance

Blade motion changes the effective frontal area of the meadow and therefore the way momentum is removed from the flow.

Edges

Transitions can concentrate change

At the boundary of a vegetation patch, sharp changes in drag may create local patterns different from those inside the meadow.

Bed

Morphology feeds back into flow

As the bed rises or scours, water depth and velocity adjust—creating a feedback that evolves through time.

Design

Stability needs spatial evidence

Mapping the full bed response supports better decisions about patch placement, coverage and hybrid-system geometry.
