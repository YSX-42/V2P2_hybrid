SUB PROJECT 02

Hybrid solution of seagrass and perched beach

Studying how a raised beach profile and living seagrass meadow can work together to dissipate waves, influence sediment and create a more adaptive nearshore system.

Research focus

Structure the profile. Let vegetation shape the flow.

This sub-project explores a hybrid system in which seagrass is paired with a perched beach—an elevated sediment profile retained within the nearshore zone.

The investigation asks how the two elements share the work of coastal protection. The beach profile can transform incoming waves, while flexible seagrass blades add resistance close to the bed. Experiments are designed to separate and then combine these effects.

HabitatLiving seagrass meadow
LandformEngineered beach profile
StatusExperimental programme

Study framework

Testing the coupled system.

01

Prepare living material

Collect and acclimatise real seagrass for controlled experiments that preserve vegetation flexibility and canopy behaviour.

02

Shape the perched beach

Construct repeatable raised beach profiles and vary water depth, sediment geometry and vegetation coverage.

03

Track waves and sediment

Measure attenuation, near-bed flow and bed response to understand the performance and stability of the combined system.

Questions guiding the work

Where does each element contribute most?

01

Wave transformation

How does the perched profile change wave height before water reaches the vegetated section?

02

Vegetation drag

How do blade motion, density and meadow length influence energy loss close to the bed?

03

Sediment stability

Where does the hybrid arrangement encourage deposition, and where might it concentrate erosion?

04

Design translation

Which combinations of profile geometry and seagrass coverage are most promising for further coastal application?
